one strip, three ways to fold it

Six entries live on a single paper strip. They are never edited or removed between modes — the strip is only folded differently, so the same six pieces answer to a new order and a new grouping each time.

Pick a mode below. Each crease marks where the strip bends; what sits on either side of a crease is what the current mode has decided belongs together.

Swipe left/right over the map, use ← → keys, or the buttons above. Modes are bounded: Prev/Next disable at the ends.

COLOPHON

An index is a bet about what belongs next to what. Shelve a book by size and it keeps company with volumes it has nothing to say to; shelve it by subject and a slim pamphlet sits beside a brick of a thing three times its height. Neither arrangement is wrong — each just answers a different question about the same shelf.

The strip here holds six fixed entries: a meridian, a datum, a horizon, a grid, a plot, a needle — fragments of how a place gets measured and marked. SCALE folds them by how large a claim each makes. SEQUENCE folds them in the order they were set down. RELATION folds them by who answers to whom.

No entry is edited or removed between folds. Order, grouping and crease positions change.
